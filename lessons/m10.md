# Module 10 — Operation Cyber Resilience (Capstone)

**Day 5 · Afternoon Session · ~4.5 hours · Hands-on lab: `labctl start m10`**

A real attack does not arrive as one neat module with a labelled answer. It hits
email, network, identity, data and people **at once**, over hours, while the
pressure mounts, the phones ring, and the information you have is incomplete and
partly contradictory. This capstone is where the whole week comes together. You
work a live, multi-vector incident against a fictional Ghanaian institution as
**both** the technical investigator *and* the decision-maker — and the thing being
assessed is not whether you can run a tool, but whether you can see the **one
adversary** behind the chaos and respond to it with discipline.

The two registers finally fuse. The **technical** work uses every instrument from
the week — Nmap and Wireshark on the live range, header forensics on the BEC
email, `jq` over the alert feed, hash cracking, and probing the bank's own AI
assistant. The **leadership** work runs alongside it in real time — declaring
severity, containing without destroying evidence, applying the deepfake protocol,
setting recovery priorities, and recording defensible decisions on a crisis clock.
By the end you will have produced a complete incident dossier and a 90-day
resilience roadmap, exactly as a real response team must.

::: outcome | By the end of this capstone you will be able to
1. Run an incident as a **crew under an Incident Commander** — the cells and their roles, a common operating picture, a battle rhythm, and the **OODA loop** under pressure.
2. **Decide on incomplete information** and avoid the cognitive traps (tunnel vision, confirmation bias, premature closure) that sink real responses.
3. **Profile the adversary** with the Diamond Model so the campaign — not just the injects — comes into focus.
4. Investigate a **live, multi-vector attack** with every tool from the week and **correlate** the injects into one campaign (Kill Chain · ATT&CK · Diamond · shared-indicator pivot) — proving the **BEC mule account, the AI's poisoned answer and the network foothold are one operation**.
5. Work the **minute-by-minute playbook** — collect-and-decide at each inject — and drive the **technical response** (isolate, eradicate, recover) without destroying evidence.
6. Run **crisis communications** across every stakeholder and address the **reporting obligations** (Act 1038 / Act 843).
7. Run a blameless **after-action review**, produce the **incident dossier** and a CSF-mapped **90-day roadmap**, and know how you're **scored** — then graduate and plan what's next.
:::

## 1 · The Orchestra, Not the Instruments

Every earlier module taught one instrument. The capstone is the orchestra. The
institution is under a **multi-vector attack** that unfolds as the eight injects
above over a simulated morning, and you work it from two sides simultaneously —
the technical investigation *and* the crisis leadership. A real response team runs
**four operational cells** in parallel, and the lab mirrors this:

- **Crisis Leadership** — declares severity, owns decisions, talks to the board.
- **Technical Investigation** — scans, captures, triages, correlates.
- **Fraud / Finance** — freezes payments, pursues recall, liaises with the bank.
- **Legal / Privacy / Comms** — reporting obligations, regulator, customers, staff.

::: key | The working method: Collect → Correlate → Decide → Record
**Collect** every artefact (alerts, the capture, the email, scan results). **Correlate**
them by shared indicators into one story. **Decide** — contain, verify, declare,
prioritise — fast and on incomplete information. **Record** every decision with a
timestamp and a rationale. In the debrief you are judged as much on the *decision
log* as on the findings.
:::

## 2 · Incident Command — Running It Under Pressure

Four cells working in parallel will **diverge** without someone holding the whole
picture. That someone is the **Incident Commander (IC)** — not necessarily the
best technician, but the person who keeps the picture whole, decides, and shields
the responders from noise and executives from panic. A **scribe** keeps the
decision log so the IC can think.

The cells feed a **common operating picture (COP)** — one shared view of what is
*known*, what is *assumed*, what is *unknown*, and what each cell is doing right
now — and the IC runs a **battle rhythm**: short, regular syncs (every 20–30
minutes) where each cell reports new facts, the COP updates, and priorities
re-set. Without that rhythm the cells duplicate work and miss the hand-offs.

Under pressure you cycle the **OODA loop** — **Observe → Orient → Decide → Act** —
fast and repeatedly. The attacker is running their own loop; whoever cycles faster
*and more accurately* wins. **Orient** — making sense of partial, contradictory
data — is the hardest step, and it is exactly where correlation lives.

### 2.1 The team — who does what (run it as a crew)

If your facilitator assigns cells, this is the crew. One person can hold more than
one role in a small class, but the **Incident Commander and the Scribe are never
the same person** — the IC must be free to think.

| Role | Owns | First move at the klaxon |
|------|------|--------------------------|
| **Incident Commander** | the whole picture, severity, decisions, the board | stand up the cells; declare severity; open the decision log |
| **Scribe** | the decision log + the common operating picture | start the timestamped log; capture every decision + rationale |
| **Technical lead(s)** | scan, capture, triage, correlate (M3/4/6) | pull the alert feed, the capture, the scan — find the foothold IP |
| **Fraud / Finance** | freeze the payment, bank recall (M5) | call the bank's fraud desk; hold the suspect transfer |
| **AI / app** | probe the assistant, quarantine the poisoned KB (M7) | ask the AI the supplier-account question; compare to the BEC |
| **Legal / Privacy / Comms** | Act 1038/843 duties, counsel, stakeholders (M8/M9) | draft the holding statement; prep the regulator notification |

::: key | The one rule that makes a team faster, not slower
**Everything flows to the Scribe, and the IC speaks for the team.** Cells don't act
on irreversible steps (isolate a server, pay/freeze money, notify a regulator)
without the IC's call — and the IC doesn't make that call without it hitting the
log. That single discipline is the difference between a coordinated crew and six
people stepping on each other.
:::

## 3 · Decision-Making Under Pressure

You will decide on **incomplete and partly wrong** information — and *waiting for
certainty is itself a decision*, usually a bad one. Two disciplines keep you sound:

**Prioritise by impact and reversibility.** Do first the things that are
high-impact and hard to undo-if-you-wait — **isolate the foothold, freeze the
payment, preserve the evidence**. Defer the low-impact and easily-reversible. A
good-enough action now beats a perfect action after the money has gone.

**Beware the cognitive traps** that sink real responses:

- **Tunnel vision / first-story bias** — latching onto the first explanation ("it's
  just a phishing email") and missing the wider campaign.
- **Confirmation bias** — seeing only the evidence that fits your theory.
- **Premature closure** — declaring it over before every foothold is found (M6) —
  and the attacker walks back in through the one you missed.
- **Normalisation of deviance** — "those alerts are always noise" (M4) — which is
  exactly how the real one slips past.

The counters are built into the command structure: the IC's outside view, the
**decision log** (which forces you to *state* your reasoning), and the capstone's
governing assumption — **treat the injects as connected until the evidence proves
otherwise.**

### 5.1 A repeatable method for correlating a campaign

Correlation isn't luck; it's a method you can run every time, using the week's
frameworks as lenses:

1. **Pivot on shared indicators.** For every new artefact ask: *have I seen this
   IP, account, domain or hash before?* The **foothold IP** and the **mule
   account** are the threads that tie the injects together.
2. **Place each event on the Kill Chain** (where in the intrusion) and tag its
   **ATT&CK** technique (what). The *gaps* then reveal the stages you haven't found
   yet — "I have delivery and C2 but no persistence; go look for it."
3. **Pivot with the Diamond Model** — from the C2 infrastructure to the adversary's
   other capability and victims.
4. **Rank indicators on the Pyramid of Pain** — block the **TTP** (dynamic-DNS
   beaconing), not just today's IP.
5. **Default to one adversary.** Treat simultaneous events as a single campaign
   until the evidence forces them apart — coincidence is rare at 09:00 on a Tuesday.

## 6 · Running the Investigation

The injects reward the exact tools you drilled. A representative command per
front:

```Terminal — work the fronts in parallel
# BEC email (M5): expose the look-alike domain and the mule account
grep -iE '^(From|Return-Path|Reply-To)' assets/payment-change.eml

# The bank's AI assistant (M7): does its poisoned KB serve the SAME account?
curl -s 10.13.10.40:8080/ask -d '{"question":"supplier bank account"}' | jq

# Correlate the alert feed (M4): which internal IP is the foothold?
jq -r '.[].data.srcip // empty' assets/capstone-alerts.json | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
jq '[.[].rule.level] | max' assets/capstone-alerts.json        # highest severity

# The network capture (M3): the C2 beacon and the exfil burst
wireshark assets/capstone-capture.pcap      # filter: dns  (C2);  then the large outbound

# The finance portal (M4): the exposed backup and a crackable admin hash
gobuster dir -u http://10.13.10.10 -w /usr/share/wordlists/dirb/common.txt
```

As you collect, keep tying findings back to the **foothold IP** — it is the
through-line that connects the privileged login, the internal scan and the
exfiltration into one intrusion.

## 7 · The Minute-by-Minute Playbook

This is the exercise as it *feels* in the room — the klaxon sounds, the injects
land on the clock, and each one forces a **collect** and a **decide**. Work it in
your cells; the Scribe times every decision.

| Clock | The inject lands | Collect (technical) | Decide / act (command) |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **T+00** | "Urgent: supplier changed bank details" email | header-forensic it (M5) → look-alike domain + **mule account** | **Hold the payment.** Don't reply. Log it. |
| **T+20** | Privileged login, odd IP, off-hours | pull the alert feed (M4) → the **foothold IP** | Flag the account; start building the COP around that IP. |
| **T+40** | That internal host is scanning the LAN | confirm the same IP in the capture/alerts (M3) | **Declare an incident.** Stand up all cells. Prepare to isolate. |
| **T+65** | Finance: "the vendor's details were changed 48h ago — a transfer is queued" | tie the change to the BEC account | **Freeze the transfer; call the bank** (golden hour, M5). |
| **T+90** | A voice note "from the CEO" orders a wire *now* | note it; don't act on it | **Refuse-and-verify:** out-of-band callback on a known number (M7). The urgency is the tell. |
| **T+120** | Large outbound to an unknown IP | find the **exfil IP** + the **C2** in the capture (M3/M6) | Now it is a **data breach** — start the Act 843/1038 clock (M8). |
| **T+145** | Core database encrypted — ransomware | identify strain; check the exfil *preceded* it (double extortion, M6) | **Isolate, do NOT power off.** Set RTO/RPO; plan restore from offline backup (M9). |
| **T+160** | The AI assistant is serving the supplier account | ask it; it returns the **same mule account** (M7) | **Proof of one campaign.** Quarantine the poisoned KB; brief the board. |

::: key | The pivot that wins the exercise
Most teams treat T+00, T+90 and T+160 as three separate problems. The teams that
*win* notice at **T+160** (or earlier) that the email's mule account, the AI's
answer and the network foothold are the **same operation** — and from that moment
they stop reacting inject-by-inject and start dismantling **one adversary's plan**.
Get to that realisation fast, and everything after it is calmer.
:::

## 8 · Running the Response (by the playbook)

Investigation without decisions is a report nobody acted on. Drive the response
exactly as the week taught:

- **Contain the foothold — isolate, do *not* power off** (M6): network-isolate
  `10.13.10.66` to stop the bleeding while preserving volatile memory evidence.
- **Refuse-and-verify the voice-note transfer** (M7): the synthetic "executive"
  wire order is actioned only after an **out-of-band callback on a known number**.
  The urgency is the manipulation.
- **Declare severity** and stand up the cells; start the **decision log**.
- **Eradicate** — close the vector, reset the cracked/stolen credentials, remove
  persistence, quarantine the poisoned AI knowledge base.
- **Recover by priority** (M9): set **RTO/RPO**, restore the ransomed database from
  a **verified-clean, offline** backup (M6's 3-2-1), validate, reconnect.
- **Report** (M8): work the Act 1038 (CSA) and Act 843 (DPC) notification questions
  with counsel.

The climax techniques to name: **T1041** (Exfiltration Over C2 Channel) and
**T1486** (Data Encrypted for Impact).

## 9 · Crisis Communications

A multi-vector incident is also a **communications crisis** — and trust is won or
lost in how you speak. Map every stakeholder and prepare what each one hears:

| Audience | Message | Via |
|----------|---------|-----|
| **Board / executives** | severity, business impact, the decision asked | the IC, directly |
| **Staff** | what to do / not do (don't action payment requests; preserve evidence) | internal, calm, clear |
| **Customers** | honest, prompt — but only confirmed facts | counsel-approved (M8) |
| **Regulators** (CSA/CERT-GH, DPC) | the required notifications, on their timelines | legal/comms cell |
| **Bank / receiving institution** | freeze & recall — *immediately* | fraud/finance cell (M5 golden hour) |
| **Press / public** | a holding statement, one spokesperson | never speculation |

**Cadence:** brief early, brief regularly, and never let silence or spin fill the
gap. One voice, one consistent message — a confident early statement that later
proves false does more damage than the breach (M8: *counsel before any external
communication*).

## 10 · The After-Action Review & the 90-Day Roadmap

The incident is not over when the systems are back — it is over after the
**after-action review (AAR, or "hotwash")**, held within days while memory is
fresh. It is where a bad day becomes a stronger institution:

- **Reconstruct the timeline** from the decision log and the evidence.
- **What went well, what didn't** — *blameless*: the goal is better systems, not
  scapegoats; people hide mistakes you punish.
- **Find the true root cause** — ask "why" five times past the symptom: *the
  exposed service? → no monthly self-scan → no asset owner → no board mandate for
  one.* The real fix is usually governance, not the symptom.
- **Turn every lesson into an owned action** — each becomes a new **control** (the
  M2 register), a **detection** (an M4 rule), a **playbook** step, or a
  **governance** item (M9), with an owner and a date.

That list *is* your **90-day resilience roadmap** — the capstone deliverable, and
the loop closing all the way back to Day 1.

**The roadmap template** — structure every lesson as *Now / Next / Later*, each
item mapped to a **NIST CSF function** with an owner and a date, so the board sees
a plan it can fund and track:

| Horizon | CSF function | Example action from this incident | Owner |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------------|-------|
| **Now (0–30d)** | Protect / Respond | enforce MFA on all remote access; dual-control + callback on payment changes | CISO / Finance |
| **Now (0–30d)** | Recover | fix and **test** the offline backup that failed the ransomware | IT Ops |
| **Next (30–60d)** | Detect | DNS-beacon + Kerberoast detections (M3/M4); ship logs off-box | SOC |
| **Next (30–60d)** | Identify | monthly self-scan + asset owners; close the exposed service's root cause | Risk |
| **Later (60–90d)** | Govern | board owns cyber; risk-appetite statement; tabletop quarterly (M9) | Board / CISO |
| **Later (60–90d)** | Protect | governed AI (quarantined KB, output checks, M7); vendor DPAs (M8) | Data/AI lead |

::: key | An incident you don't learn from, you will repeat
The teams that improve aren't the ones that never get hit — they are the ones whose
every incident ends in a blameless AAR that changes a control, a detection or a
governance gap. The roadmap is how this week's crisis makes next year's smaller —
and it is the single most valuable thing you hand your board.
:::

## 11 · How You're Scored — the Debrief Rubric

The capstone is not graded on tool output alone. `labctl verify m10` checks your
**findings**; the facilitator debrief weighs how you *ran* it. Know the four
dimensions, because they are how real responders are judged too:

| Dimension | Weight | Excellent | Weak |
|-----------|-------:|-----------|------|
| **Investigation** | 25% | every artefact found with evidence (email, foothold IP, C2, exfil, hash, AI answer) | partial findings, no evidence trail |
| **Correlation** | 30% | proves email + AI + network are **one campaign**, with the method | treats injects as separate problems |
| **Decisions** | 25% | isolate-not-poweroff, freeze the payment, **verify the voice note out-of-band**, prioritise by impact/reversibility | actioned the deepfake, powered off, waited for certainty |
| **Command & comms** | 20% | clean **decision log**, clear COP, honest stakeholder comms, blameless AAR | no log, confused comms, blame |

::: key | The decision log is scored as heavily as the findings
Two teams can find the same evidence; the one that **recorded what it decided,
when and why** — and verified the deepfake, and isolated instead of powering off —
is the one that would survive the real incident *and* the regulator afterward. In
the debrief, "show me your decision log" is the first question. Write it as you go,
not from memory at the end.
:::

## Common mistakes

::: warning | What trips teams up
- **Treating injects in isolation.** The whole point is that they are one
  campaign — correlate by shared indicators (the foothold IP, the shared account).
- **Powering off the foothold** to "stop it" — isolate instead, to preserve
  evidence.
- **Actioning the voice-note transfer** because it sounds urgent and real — verify
  out-of-band on a known number.
- **Trusting the AI's answer** because "the system said so" — it was poisoned; that
  is the trap.
- **No decision log.** In the debrief you must show *what you decided, when, and
  why.*
:::

## Key takeaways

::: key | Carry these out of the capstone
- A real attack is **multi-vector and simultaneous** — run it as a **crew under an
  Incident Commander** (each cell a clear role, everything to the Scribe), with a
  common operating picture and a battle rhythm, cycling the **OODA loop** faster and
  more accurately than the attacker.
- **Profile the adversary** (Diamond Model) so you fight the *plan*, not the injects;
  **decide on incomplete information**, beware the cognitive traps, and **assume the
  injects are connected until proven otherwise.**
- The master skill is **correlation** — a repeatable method (shared-indicator pivot ·
  Kill Chain · ATT&CK · Diamond) that turns scattered alerts into **one campaign**.
  "The system said so" is not verification when the system is poisoned.
- Work the **minute-by-minute playbook**: at each inject, *collect* then *decide* —
  **isolate (don't power off), freeze the payment, verify the voice note out-of-band,
  set RTO/RPO, log every decision** — and run honest **crisis comms**.
- You are **scored** on investigation + correlation + decisions + command/comms, and
  the **decision log** counts as much as the findings.
- Close the loop with a **blameless after-action review** → a CSF-mapped **90-day
  roadmap**. Then **graduate** and apply one control at home this month.
- Climax techniques: **T1041** (exfiltration), **T1486** (ransomware).
:::

## Check yourself

1. **The BEC email, a privileged login, an internal scan and an exfiltration all
   involve one IP. What does that tell you?**
   → They are one campaign, not four incidents — the shared IP is the foothold
   tying the attack together. Correlate; don't triage separately.

2. **You ask the bank's AI assistant for the supplier account and it returns the
   exact number the BEC email demanded. What have you just proven?**
   → That the phishing and the AI compromise are the **same campaign** — the
   knowledge base was poisoned to make the fraud validate itself. "The system said
   so" is not verification.

3. **A voice note "from the CEO" demands an urgent transfer mid-incident. Action
   it?**
   → No — verify out-of-band on a known number first. It is a likely deepfake; the
   urgency is the manipulation.

4. **You contain the foothold host — isolate or power off?**
   → **Isolate** from the network; powering off destroys volatile memory evidence.

5. **Name the ATT&CK techniques for the exfiltration and the ransomware.**
   → **T1041** (Exfiltration Over C2 Channel) and **T1486** (Data Encrypted for
   Impact).

6. **Mid-incident you have only partial, partly-contradictory information. Name two
   cognitive traps to guard against, and the discipline that keeps you moving.**
   → Any two of: **tunnel vision / first-story bias, confirmation bias, premature
   closure, normalisation of deviance**. The discipline: **prioritise by impact and
   reversibility** and act on good-enough information — waiting for certainty is
   itself a (bad) decision.

7. **The systems are restored and the attacker is out. Is the incident over? What
   happens next, and what does it produce?**
   → Not yet — you hold a **blameless after-action review**: reconstruct the
   timeline, find the true root cause (five whys), and turn each lesson into an
   owned control / detection / governance action. That list is the **90-day
   resilience roadmap**.

8. **Two teams find the exact same evidence, but one scores far higher. Why?**
   → The capstone scores **correlation, decisions and command/comms**, not just
   findings — and the **decision log** counts as much as the evidence. The team that
   proved one campaign, verified the deepfake, isolated-not-powered-off and recorded
   why wins.

9. **Why bother profiling the adversary during a live incident instead of just
   working the alerts?**
   → Attribution (Diamond Model) lets you fight the *plan*, not the symptoms: once
   you orient on "a hybrid crew that corrupts the victim's own tools," the injects
   snap into one campaign, you distrust every source they touched, and you can
   predict the end-game (exfil → ransomware).

## Glossary

- **Multi-vector attack** — one campaign using several techniques at once.
- **Inject** — a scripted event introduced during an exercise.
- **Incident Commander (IC)** — the person who owns the response, keeps the picture whole and decides.
- **Common operating picture (COP)** — one shared view of known/assumed/unknown and who is doing what.
- **Battle rhythm** — the regular sync cadence the IC runs to keep the cells aligned.
- **OODA loop** — Observe → Orient → Decide → Act, cycled fast under pressure.
- **Correlation** — linking separate events via shared indicators into one story.
- **Foothold** — the attacker's established position inside the network.
- **Operational cell** — a focused response team (leadership, technical, fraud, legal/comms).
- **Decision log** — timestamped record of crisis decisions and rationale.
- **Crisis communications** — honest, coordinated, one-voice messaging to every stakeholder.
- **After-action review (hotwash)** — the blameless post-incident retro that produces the lessons/roadmap.
- **RTO / RPO** — recovery time / recovery point objectives (Module 9).
- **Kill Chain / ATT&CK** — where in the intrusion / which specific technique.
- **T1041 / T1486** — exfiltration over C2 / data encrypted for impact.
- **Diamond Model** — the CTI frame (adversary · capability · infrastructure · victim) used to profile the campaign.
- **Scribe** — the role that owns the decision log and the COP (never also the IC).
- **Minute-by-minute playbook** — the collect-then-decide action for each timed inject.
- **90-day resilience roadmap** — the AAR's lessons as Now/Next/Later actions mapped to the NIST CSF, each owned.
- **Blameless** — an AAR culture focused on fixing systems, not punishing people.
