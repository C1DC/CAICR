# Module 10 — Operation Cyber Resilience (Capstone)

**Day 5 · Afternoon · Hands-on lab: `labctl start m10`**

In one sentence: a real attack doesn't arrive as one neat module — it hits
email, network, identity, data and people at once, and this capstone is where you
put the whole week together under pressure.

By the end you will be able to:
- investigate a live, multi-vector attack across all the tools from the week;
- correlate separate pieces of evidence into one coherent attack story;
- drive both the technical response and the leadership decisions;
- make defensible calls fast, and record them.

---

## The big picture

Every earlier module taught one instrument. The capstone is the orchestra. A
fictional Ghanaian institution is under a **multi-vector attack** that unfolds as
**eight injects** over a simulated morning, and you work it from two sides at
once:

- **Technical investigation** — Nmap the range, read the capture in Wireshark,
  trace the BEC email's headers, triage the Wazuh alerts, recover and decode
  evidence.
- **Leadership & response** — declare severity, contain without destroying
  evidence, apply the deepfake protocol, set recovery priorities, and handle the
  Act 843/1038 reporting questions.

The single skill the capstone really tests is **correlation**: recognising that
the supplier email, the odd privileged login, the internal scan, the voice-note
transfer request, the data exfiltration and the database going dark are *not six
problems* — they are one adversary moving through the kill chain.

## An analogy

Think of a hospital trauma team when a bus crash arrives. No single doctor owns
it. One commander triages and directs; specialists work airway, bleeding and
fractures in parallel; someone logs every decision and drug. The week trained
each specialist skill; the capstone is the trauma call where they work *together*,
fast, under a commander, with the clock running and lives (here, an institution)
on the line.

## How it actually works

The eight injects map straight onto the week:

| Inject | Event | Module skill |
|--------|-------|--------------|
| T+00 | Supplier payment-change email (urgent) | M5 header forensics |
| T+20 | Privileged login from an unusual IP, off-hours | M3/M4 identity + SIEM |
| T+40 | Internal host scanning the network | M3 Nmap / lateral movement |
| T+65 | Vendor bank details changed 48h ago; near-miss transfer | M5 BEC / M2 process |
| T+90 | Synthetic executive *voice note* orders a wire transfer | M7 deepfake protocol |
| T+120 | Data exfiltration — large outbound to an unknown IP | M3/M6 network + evidence |
| T+145 | Critical database offline (ransomware trigger) | M6 IR lifecycle |
| T+160 | AI assistant leaks data via an injected document | M7 prompt injection |

Four **operational cells** run in parallel, exactly as a real crisis team does:
**Crisis Leadership, Technical Investigation, Fraud/Finance, Legal/Privacy/Comms.**

Working method:

1. **Collect** — pull every piece: alerts, the packet capture, the email, scan
   results.
2. **Correlate** — tie them with shared indicators. The same foothold IP appears
   in the privileged login, the internal scan *and* the exfiltration — that's your
   through-line.
3. **Decide** — contain the foothold (**isolate, don't power off** — preserve
   evidence), refuse-and-verify the voice-note transfer (callback on a known
   number), declare severity, set recovery priorities (RTO/RPO).
4. **Record** — a decision log with timestamps and rationale.

Map the climax to ATT&CK: exfiltration is **T1041**; the ransomware is **T1486**
(Data Encrypted for Impact).

## Ghana in focus

Every inject is drawn from real Ghanaian incident patterns you studied this
week — the GCB-style BEC, the SIM/MoMo and deepfake-voice fraud, the hospital
ransomware with its backup and power-off failures, the exposed-service foothold.
The capstone deliberately fuses them because *that is how real attacks on
Ghanaian institutions actually arrive* — not as a tidy single technique, but as a
campaign that mixes fraud, intrusion and social engineering to maximise damage
and confusion. Resilience is the ability to keep thinking clearly while all of it
happens at once.

## Why it matters

- Real incidents are messy and simultaneous; practising the *integration* is what
  separates a team that contains a breach from one that's overwhelmed by it.
- Correlation under pressure is the hardest and most valuable SOC skill — and the
  one you can only build by doing.
- The habit of **deciding and logging** fast, on incomplete information, is what
  leadership in a crisis actually looks like.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating injects in isolation.** The whole point is they're one campaign;
  correlate by shared indicators.
- **Powering off the foothold** to "stop it" — isolate instead, to preserve
  evidence (M6).
- **Actioning the voice-note transfer** because it sounds urgent and real — verify
  out-of-band (M7).
- **No decision log.** In the debrief you must be able to show *what you decided,
  when, and why.*

## Key takeaways

- A real attack is **multi-vector and simultaneous** — the capstone integrates the
  whole week.
- The master skill is **correlation**: shared indicators (like one foothold IP)
  turn scattered alerts into one story.
- Respond by the playbook: **isolate (don't power off), verify out-of-band,
  declare severity, set RTO/RPO, log decisions.**
- ATT&CK at the climax: **T1041** (exfiltration), **T1486** (ransomware).

## Check yourself

1. **The BEC email, a privileged login, an internal scan and an exfiltration all
   involve one IP. What does that tell you?**
   → They're one campaign, not four incidents — the shared IP is the foothold
   tying the attack together. Correlate, don't triage them separately.

2. **A voice note "from the CEO" demands an urgent transfer mid-incident. Action
   it?**
   → No — verify out-of-band on a known number first. It's a likely deepfake
   (M7); urgency is the manipulation.

3. **You contain the foothold host. Isolate or power off?**
   → Isolate from the network — powering off destroys volatile memory evidence
   (M6).

4. **Name the ATT&CK techniques for the exfiltration and the ransomware.**
   → T1041 (Exfiltration Over C2 Channel) and T1486 (Data Encrypted for Impact).

## Glossary

- **Multi-vector attack** — one campaign using several techniques at once.
- **Inject** — a scripted event introduced during an exercise.
- **Correlation** — linking separate events via shared indicators into one story.
- **Operational cell** — a focused response team (leadership, technical,
  fraud, legal/comms).
- **Decision log** — timestamped record of crisis decisions and rationale.
- **T1041 / T1486** — ATT&CK for exfiltration over C2 / data encrypted for impact.
