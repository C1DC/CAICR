# Module 10 — Operation Cyber Resilience (Capstone)

**Day 5 · Afternoon · Hands-on lab: `labctl start m10`**

A real attack does not arrive as one neat module. It hits email, network,
identity, data and people at once, over hours, while the pressure mounts and the
information is incomplete. This capstone is where the whole week comes together —
you work a live, multi-vector incident as both the technical investigator and the
decision-maker.

By the end you will be able to:
- investigate a live, multi-vector attack across every tool from the week;
- correlate scattered evidence into one coherent attack story;
- drive both the technical response and the leadership decisions;
- make defensible calls fast, on incomplete information, and record them.

---

## 1. The big picture: the orchestra, not the instruments

Every earlier module taught one instrument. The capstone is the orchestra. A
fictional Ghanaian institution is under a **multi-vector attack** that unfolds as
**eight injects** over a simulated morning, and you work it from two sides at
once:

- **Technical investigation** — Nmap the range, read the capture in Wireshark,
  trace the BEC email's headers, triage the Wazuh alerts, recover and decode
  evidence.
- **Leadership & response** — declare severity, contain without destroying
  evidence, apply the deepfake protocol, set recovery priorities, handle the Act
  843/1038 reporting questions.

The single skill the capstone tests is **correlation**: recognising that the
supplier email, the odd privileged login, the internal scan, the voice-note
transfer request, the data exfiltration and the database going dark are *not six
problems* — they are one adversary moving through the **Cyber Kill Chain**, and
every stage maps to **MITRE ATT&CK**.

## 2. An analogy

A hospital trauma team when a bus crash arrives. No single doctor owns it: one
commander triages and directs; specialists work airway, bleeding and fractures in
parallel; someone logs every decision and every drug. The week trained each
specialist skill. The capstone is the trauma call where they work *together*,
fast, under a commander, with the clock running and an institution on the line.

## 3. How it works — the eight injects, mapped

The injects map straight onto the week and onto the Kill Chain:

| Inject | Event | Kill Chain / module | ATT&CK |
|--------|-------|---------------------|--------|
| T+00 | Supplier payment-change email (urgent) | Delivery · M5 | T1566 |
| T+20 | Privileged login, unusual IP, off-hours | Exploitation · M3/M4 | T1078 |
| T+40 | Internal host scanning the network | Recon/Lateral · M3 | T1046 |
| T+65 | Vendor bank details changed 48h ago; near-miss transfer | Actions · M5/M2 | T1565 |
| T+90 | Synthetic executive *voice note* orders a wire transfer | Delivery · M7 | T1656 |
| T+120 | Data exfiltration — large outbound to unknown IP | Actions · M3/M6 | T1041 |
| T+145 | Critical database offline (ransomware trigger) | Actions · M6 | T1486 |
| T+160 | AI assistant leaks data via an injected document | Execution · M7 | T1059 |

Four **operational cells** run in parallel, exactly as a real crisis team does:
**Crisis Leadership, Technical Investigation, Fraud/Finance, Legal/Privacy/Comms.**

The working method:

1. **Collect** — every piece: alerts, the packet capture, the email, scan results.
2. **Correlate** — tie them with shared indicators. The same foothold IP appears
   in the privileged login, the internal scan *and* the exfiltration — that is
   your through-line.
3. **Decide** — contain the foothold (**isolate, don't power off** — preserve
   evidence, Module 6), refuse-and-verify the voice-note transfer (callback on a
   known number, Module 7), declare severity, set recovery priorities (RTO/RPO,
   Module 9).
4. **Record** — a decision log with timestamps and rationale.

## 4. Scenarios — because this is how real attacks actually arrive

Every inject is drawn from a real pattern you studied this week — the GCB-style
BEC, SIM/MoMo and deepfake-voice fraud, the hospital ransomware with its backup
and power-off failures, the exposed-service foothold. Internationally, the same
*combination* is the modern norm: the **2016 Bangladesh Bank** heist fused
intrusion, fraud and timing; **NotPetya** fused worm propagation with destructive
impact; the **Arup 2024** deepfake fused social engineering with finance process
failure. Real campaigns mix fraud, intrusion and social engineering precisely to
maximise damage and confusion. Resilience is the ability to keep thinking clearly
while all of it happens at once.

## 5. Why it matters

- Real incidents are messy and simultaneous; practising the *integration* is what
  separates a team that contains a breach from one overwhelmed by it.
- Correlation under pressure is the hardest and most valuable SOC skill — and the
  one you can only build by doing.
- The habit of **deciding and logging** fast, on incomplete information, is what
  leadership in a crisis actually looks like.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Treating injects in isolation** — the whole point is that they are one
  campaign; correlate by shared indicators.
- **Powering off the foothold** to "stop it" — isolate instead, to preserve
  evidence.
- **Actioning the voice-note transfer** because it sounds urgent and real — verify
  out-of-band.
- **No decision log** — in the debrief you must show *what you decided, when, and
  why.*

## Key takeaways

- A real attack is **multi-vector and simultaneous** — the capstone integrates the
  whole week along the **Kill Chain** and **ATT&CK**.
- The master skill is **correlation**: a shared indicator (one foothold IP) turns
  scattered alerts into one story.
- Respond by the playbook: **isolate (don't power off), verify out-of-band, declare
  severity, set RTO/RPO, log decisions.**
- Climax techniques: **T1041** (exfiltration), **T1486** (ransomware).

## Check yourself

1. **The BEC email, a privileged login, an internal scan and an exfiltration all
   involve one IP. What does that tell you?**
   → They are one campaign, not four incidents — the shared IP is the foothold
   tying the attack together. Correlate; don't triage them separately.

2. **A voice note "from the CEO" demands an urgent transfer mid-incident. Action
   it?**
   → No — verify out-of-band on a known number first. It is a likely deepfake
   (Module 7); the urgency is the manipulation.

3. **You contain the foothold host — isolate or power off?**
   → Isolate from the network; powering off destroys volatile memory evidence
   (Module 6).

4. **Name the ATT&CK techniques for the exfiltration and the ransomware.**
   → T1041 (Exfiltration Over C2 Channel) and T1486 (Data Encrypted for Impact).

## Glossary

- **Multi-vector attack** — one campaign using several techniques at once.
- **Inject** — a scripted event introduced during an exercise.
- **Correlation** — linking separate events via shared indicators into one story.
- **Kill Chain / ATT&CK** — where in the intrusion / which specific technique.
- **Operational cell** — a focused response team (leadership, technical, fraud,
  legal/comms).
- **Decision log** — timestamped record of crisis decisions and rationale.
- **T1041 / T1486** — exfiltration over C2 / data encrypted for impact.
